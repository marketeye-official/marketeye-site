What you can actually do

You can't control the market. You can learn to read it.

The year the money runs out isn't fixed. It's what one set of assumptions produces.

Most of it is out of your hands

Interest rates, inflation and government policy are set by other people. A plan that leans on them leans on their decisions.

One part you can learn

How markets move is something you can learn, and nobody's permission is required. That's a skill, not a shortcut.

Two paths

Self-taught, or taught in order.

Most people start in the middle: a chart, an indicator, a setup someone posted online. They never learn what sits underneath it. That's the difference — and it isn't talent.

Piecing it together alone

  • Start at charts and indicators, skip what a market actually is
  • Collect setups without the structure that explains why they work
  • Mistake noise for signal, because nothing distinguishes them yet
  • Risk becomes an afterthought instead of the first question

Learning it in order

  • Start with how price forms and who moves it
  • Understand the instrument before opening a platform
  • Read structure, liquidity, and session levels deliberately
  • Plan the risk before the entry — every time, as a rule

The question everyone asks

"Isn't it a bit late to be learning this?"

It deserves a straight answer rather than a reassuring one.

Reading a market is not a young person's game. It rewards patience, doubt, and the willingness to walk away from a bad trade — and those come with time. Module 4 spends a whole lesson on when not to trade. That instinct is the hard part to teach.
